## 5. Prospective control across genuinely future confirmations

Historical r1 and CORE intervals used their own nominal 95% procedures. The
research sequence above had **no preallocated shared total alpha**. Calling the
past tests fractions of a newly invented budget would be false. Preserve their
reported nominal intervals and failed/pass statuses with that multiplicity
limitation. A prospective family cannot retroactively provide lifetime coverage
or validate historical selection.

For review, one possible **future-only** rule is a cumulative family budget
of 0.05, with chronological study index j starting at 1 and

`alpha_j = 0.05 / 2^j`, so `sum(j >= 1, alpha_j) = 0.05`.

For a **single overall claim covering both user goals**, each study would
precommit two-sided intervals with nominal confidence `1-alpha_j` for all its
fixed primary contrasts: the six core contrasts and the separately specified
dynamic-over-best-static contrasts. It would require **every lower bound > 0**,
together with unchanged engineering gates. For example the first study would
use 97.5% two-sided intervals, not another nominal 95% retry. This
is an intersection-union test: when the conjunction is false, at least one
required effect is nonpositive, and wrongly declaring the whole conjunction
requires rejecting that true component null. A Bonferroni division by six is
not needed inside the core conjunction, nor is a division by the total number
of components needed for the single combined conjunction. It does **not** give
simultaneous coverage for all displayed intervals or license picking a
favorable subset.
Using the lower end of a valid two-sided `1-alpha_j` interval is conservative
for the corresponding one-sided positive-effect test.

If a future protocol instead permits **separate confirmation declarations**
for core benefit and dynamic benefit, it must allocate each study's error
budget between those two claims prospectively, for example `alpha_j/2` each
with two-sided confidence `1-alpha_j/2` within each claim's conjunction. Then
the per-study union bound covers falsely declaring either claim. Passing only
core does not establish dynamic benefit, and the six-core IUT argument alone
does not control two separately selectable declarations. The choice between a
single combined declaration and separately allocated claims requires review
before the family starts; it cannot be decided after seeing which succeeds.

With valid study-level tests, the union bound limits the probability of any
false conjunction in this **future family** by the sum of its allocated
levels; it does not require independence between its six endpoints. A rigorous
sequential guarantee also needs component inference valid conditional on the
prior development/selection history and genuinely fresh confirmation evidence.
An approximate small-seed percentile bootstrap is not automatically a
finite-sample level guarantee. The sampling model, inferential method, seed and
source/cohort resampling, dependence, tail accuracy and Monte Carlo precision
therefore require independent statistical review before any registration.
Do not merely change percentile labels on the old 2000-replicate procedure,
particularly as later allocated tails become smaller.

The family must include every later confirmation of this same scientific
claims after its prospectively declared start. A new branch, protocol name,
codec, dataset salt or nominally new project does not reset j or replenish
alpha. A study that starts but fails readiness, construction, is interrupted,
or yields a partial test should retain its reserved allocation conservatively;
any alternative reclamation rule must itself be reviewed and frozen before the
family starts, never after observing an outcome. Publish every attempt and
allocation. This draft creates no such family ledger or allocation.

A second review option is **one fixed, stricter future confirmation and one
separately budgeted independent replication**, with fresh sources/models and
all results published, requiring both to support the claim. Its total
prospective error rule, inference and costs still need explicit approval;
the word “replication” does not permit unbudgeted repeated 95% tests. These are
alternative draft designs, not permission to choose whichever succeeds.
