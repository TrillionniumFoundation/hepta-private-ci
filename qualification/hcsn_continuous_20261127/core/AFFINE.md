The stationary barrier is affine in principal at fixed coupon and other coefficients. At a fixed live log margin, both A7 equity and the monetary first-hit shortage have only affine principal coefficients. Calculate the intercept and slope at each atom once, then recover both prices exactly for each new principal. This requires frozen law coefficients and a fixed grid. It does not linearize the state transition, the risk-vs-log-margin law, or the entire neural network. The ROG/reference and local CPU implementations keep ordinary BP. On a one-thread, 32-record, actual FordA batch (two recurrent layers, twenty steps, four hidden units per layer), an alternating seven-step cold check gave warm median 0.09275 s with the native prices and 0.06405 s with the affine evaluation. Prices differed by at most 8.53e−14, principal gradients by 1.07e−14 and trained parameters after seven AdamW steps by 1.46e−16. These timings describe an optimization of this implementation.

The independent local-credit counterexample fixes every local input, the boundary, priced channels, spike flow, four horizon probes, target and forecast. Changing only a downstream sign and preserving the forecast by centering gives opposite analytic loss gradients on the same sender weight, ±0.258213. Two forward perturbations recover each sign. Thus the local solution alone cannot select a universally correct hidden update. Access to downstream weights, interventions or a task-specific target supplies the missing credit.
