# Finite-maturity funding and two endogenous walls

The fitted one-year rolling-debt model gives fundamental-state thresholds 69.76317 and 71.33297 at transition intensities 0.12 and 0.16, with liquidity premiums 0.01 and 0.04. In the constant-friction analytic He–Xiong model the two independent thresholds are 67.44152 and 74.52183. Switching moves the quiet-regime threshold upward and the stressed-regime threshold downward for these parameters. Periodic issuance proceeds depend on both states through the killed finite-maturity bond, including a regime jump into the stopping region.

## Equations and recovery choice

Write the joint generator of log fundamental state and liquidity regime as L. In the continuation region, bond face value p=P/m, coupon c=C/m, and remaining maturity t give ∂ₜd=Ld−(r+λᵢ)d+c. At zero remaining maturity a live bond pays p. Default pays αV/m immediately and fixes the bond value subsequently in the time-to-maturity calculation. Live equity solves (r−L)E=δV−(1−π)C+d(V,m)−p. Equity is zero on the stopping region, nonnegative on its complement, and has zero right derivative at a regular diffusion boundary. When a liquidity jump enters the other stopping set, recovery uses the fundamental value at the jump. The stochastic liquidity extension adopts this explicit convention. The single-regime original has the stationary closed form. This extension is a numerical, differentiable coupled solver.

## Independent calculations

1. On fixed log-space grids, solve the bond with two half backward-Euler steps and Crank–Nicolson to maturity; independently verify with an augmented matrix semigroup. Solve the equity obstacle by policy iteration and feed each implied stopping set back to the bond. The discrete outer fixed point can depend on initialization by a grid cell. The archive retains this outcome, multiple starts, 161–1281 nodes, 48–384 time steps and maturity/rate/domain sensitivity.
2. Place both wall contacts on a nonuniform grid with two independently movable knots. For any candidate pair, reprice the bond, solve the equity continuation problem and differentiate the local cubic interpolant in log fundamental value from the live side. Solve both smooth-fit residuals simultaneously. On refinements the two fitted walls are (69.80105,71.32150), (69.77077,71.33124) and (69.76317,71.33297). Two widely separated starting pairs at the last resolution agree within 5e−10 in value. The maximum smooth-fit residual there is 5.7e−9 and the largest live-equity equation residual is 1.41e−10. Neither contact convergence nor this local Jacobian proves global uniqueness of the continuum equilibrium. The uncoupled finest-grid walls differ from the analytic formula by about −0.0125 and −0.0107 on the finite domain.
3. Conditional on fixed fitted walls, simulate 300,000 exact-clock Brownian paths per cell across 24 starting-value/regime/law combinations. Draw each regime holding time and its Gaussian endpoint. Given a positive endpoint, bridge crossing occurs with probability exp(−2xy/(σ²h)); for a crossed segment, conditional U=τ/(h−τ) is inverse Gaussian with mean x/|y| and shape x²/(σ²h). Discount coupon, recovery and terminal principal at their actual times. Independent prices at V=100 are 60.61654±0.01005 and 58.93800±0.00994 (one standard error), against grid values 60.61088 and 58.92403. The maximum absolute standardized deviation across stochastic cells is 1.407. The output separates diffusion hits, regime-jump hits and maturity. This validation tests debt conditional on the fitted thresholds; equilibrium wall uncertainty has a separate refinement check.
4. Complex-step differentiation of the smooth-fit residual produces the two-by-two wall Jacobian and four parameter derivatives. Solve their implicit linear system and propagate arbitrary vector–Jacobian products through a custom CPU float64 PyTorch operation. At the finest tested grid, the sensitivities to (λ₀,λ₁,q₀₁,q₁₀) are approximately (159.31,74.82,13.11,−6.17) and (110.08,129.41,8.81,−11.24). Across six perturbation directions at three resolutions, the greatest relative discrepancy with independently refitted two-sided differences is 2.7e−6, using max(1,‖directional response‖∞) as denominator. The smallest tested perturbations leave the ordering and contact topology unchanged. On the medium grid the PyTorch gradient of log(B₁/B₀) is (−0.74000,0.74306,−0.06429,−0.06920). Degenerate/equal thresholds and state-order changes need separate derivatives.

## Neural interpretation

A receiver supplied with current liquidity-like state and learned transition rates can produce a continuous threshold and propagate an ordinary first-order gradient through this fitted equilibrium. This supports either a parameterized cell with slow equilibrium updates or a validated interpolation table. It is substantially more computation per equilibrium than the single-regime closed form. No large-network speed or task advantage follows from the local gradient test. State hitting, physical reset and refractory mass must still be specified by the neural network.
