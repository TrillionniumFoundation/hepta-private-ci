# Can observed dual prices identify and directly edit the state of one literal neuron?

For the original constant positive-friction He–Xiong law, one deterministic, active neuron has two coordinates: log margin x=log(V/B(P)) and local principal P. Coupon and all other coefficients are held fixed. Test invertibility of equity E(x,P) combined with hit probability F(x,T), and separately combined with monetary default loss L(x,P). This differs from identifying an unknown population mixture or recovering an entire trained network. Affinity in P at fixed x makes each price a linear function of P, even though x depends on V and P. The risk-probability route admits scalar monotone inversion followed by a single linear solve; report signs, domains, conditioning and failures.

Use 2,048 reproducible interior points in x∈[.005,.65], P∈[.70,1.30] times nominal, the fixed original constants r=.08, δ=.02, σ=.23, maturity 1, loss premium .01, and prediction horizons .05,.25,1. Replay in float64 and under a cast of the observed outputs to float32, bfloat16, and an 8-bit bounded uniform channel; report saturation/reconstructed coordinates/forward residuals separately. Retain a stress grid approaching the wall and far above it. For monetary loss, scan for sign changes after eliminating P, refine all candidates in the declared rectangular domain and record multiple roots or poor numerical conditioning rather than silently choosing the original state. For a finite batch of exact target states, recover both local input preactivations and write two actual weight matrices with a constrained linear system retaining an explicitly protected anchor set. Use fresh unrelated queries to evaluate off-anchor drift. Compare exact local update with a same-site iterative solver including all inverse and replay costs. Never use a target state's unknown coordinates in the inverse, except to generate synthetic targets and evaluate recovery. This is a state observability/editing test with a specified internal target. It does not supply freely available hidden targets for general model pretraining.
