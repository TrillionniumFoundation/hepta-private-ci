# Native CPU adjoints for the continuous SHD cell

The original implementation materializes two nested interpolations for a 4×5 transition for every population unit and physical frame. The compact CPU kernel computes the same quantities and differentiates transport, the wall, risk/equity channels, the three future-horizon probes and trainable output mixing. The PyTorch projection, feedback, recurrence and tagging of physical time stay in the graph.

On 24 paired full classification minibatch updates (48 real SHD examples, 28 frames, two 48-neuron layers, three seeds), V1 had a median paired speed ratio of 2.98 relative to the original eager implementation. CPU medians were 298.2 versus 97.7 ms. V2, which computes wall and interpolation-coordinate arithmetic in the reference tensor backend, had median paired speed ratio 2.65 (312.96 versus 113.48 ms), with an observed ratio of 1.89–4.09. First-time compilation/loading took 2.30 s and 3.38 s wall time in the two panels. The clock covers forward, backward, gradient clipping and the AdamW update in a single-thread experiment. Optimizer object construction and dataset preparation are outside the paired update timer. This speed ratio neither compares to Transformers nor proves less time to hit a task accuracy.

Across 36 real-input checks per implementation (four physics variants × three seeds × three amplitude scales), V1 maximum logit discrepancy was 1.12e−7. For V2, across all input and parameter gradient tensors, the largest absolute entry discrepancy was 1.93e−6 and the largest relative L2 discrepancy was 0.0030. In an exactly contacted single-step set, V1's maximum literal previous-wall VJP discrepancy was 2.4256, while V2 reduced it to 0.00000573 by selecting the original interpolation cell. At off-knot points all three double-precision central wall checks agreed with the eager double derivative to better than 1e−8, and with the native V1 float32 derivative to better than 4e−6. A learned recurrent solution evaluated in float32 may visit different cells after accumulated rounding, including under V2. The raw records make this testable without asserting exact bitwise training identity. Separate time-to-validation experiments and cold tests use actual trained weights.
