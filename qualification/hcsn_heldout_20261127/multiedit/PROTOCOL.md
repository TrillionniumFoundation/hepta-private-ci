# Paired trainable two-layer HCSN editing on new AudioMNIST speakers

This forward experiment builds on the separately sealed receiver-only study. All choices below precede running any AudioMNIST model. Source selection and the full participant and recording rules are fixed in COHORT.md. Three historical Arabic speech weight seeds 107, 211 and 431 anchor separate deep parents. The earlier FSDD/Arabic receiver results prompted a new-site and stronger-baseline test. They provide no evidence about AudioMNIST outcomes.

## Genuine stack, available weights and downstream objectives

Make two independently stored physical N32 HCSN layers per parent initialized from copies of its historical N32 weights. Each layer has its own initial distribution and causal clock. Repeat each six-frame MFCC twice to give 12 ticks. Layer B receives only previous-tick centered boundary messages from A through a fixed 32-to-13 orthonormal projection, PCG64 seed 19939, normalized by projected feature mean and per-channel standard deviation (floor .001) from the 440 old training examples alone and clamped to ±5. The first delivered message at tick zero is all zero before normalization. Layer B sees no raw speech or first-layer class logits. Causal first-layer boundary projection may use the equivalent recurrence during fitting; full simulation of both physical layers, without pruning, is mandatory for final cold evaluation. Compare complete pooled features on genuine train rows before using the fast representation, tolerance max absolute <=1e-5 plus 1e-5 times maximum full feature magnitude. Second-layer readout concatenates its mean 80-component receiver features across 12 ticks and its final 80 components. The entire readout has 160 predictors. In each layer allow changes in all active sender input coefficients and biases (224), sender-to-sender boundary weights (256), receiver boundary weights (512) and the 32 cost coordinates. This is 1,024 per layer and 2,048 jointly. All other weights, the bridge and input preprocessing are frozen. The task labels remain digits 0–9.

Train the old task's ten-way ridge readout on the new stack using original Arabic training recordings and choose a ridge penalty on the old development split. Freeze that head for all interventions. Separately fit the new AudioMNIST head using only target training recordings. Every encoder candidate, including unchanged weights, receives the same full new-head fitting procedure, using per-feature train mean and standard deviation with floor 1e-4, centered one-hot ridge, and penalty grid [.001,.01,.1,1] on the mean squared-loss convention (normal matrix divided by n). New-head selection is part of development selection. Raw flattened speech ridge gives a cheap reference. Access to language/domain identity is assumed when choosing the head and preprocessing. This compares adaptation with a new task head, not a unified language-agnostic ten-way decoder.

## Forward editing

Start the proposal from the zero-update parent. Fit provisional target ridge (.1) on support only. Use 60 support and 60 query AudioMNIST examples, six per digit by deterministic hash, from disjoint groups comprising the first 15 and next 15 training speakers; independently select six old Arabic calibration examples per digit. For basis acquisition, use the 60 new query and 60 old training samples. Use the provisional readout's class residuals (onehot minus predicted softmax of logits multiplied by 5) to weight each training sequence's local keys and sender/receiver preactivations, averaged across slow clock steps. Get a residual-directed input, sender recurrence, receiver recurrence and cost direction for each digit in each layer. Shrink each input-key direction by the inverse old key second moment plus 0.1 times its average diagonal, with an absolute numerical floor. Form unit-norm rank-one motor/key directions for affine maps, and normalize the cost direction separately. Project the ten resulting joint directions plus six seeded Gaussian joint completions to an orthonormal 16-dimensional encoder subspace, PCG64 seed 12630+parent. Only real forward values are used to acquire the basis. Record numerical rank and both layers' candidate distances.

For each of 16 directions make two full joint physical perturbations of norm .05. Refit the provisional target head at every perturbation on the same support points. Score the new query through this refit and the old examples through the retained old head. Use the central difference responses of centered logits. Fit a single regularized least-squares step on desired new logit shift onehot minus the provisional ridge regression predictions, centered, with zero desired old shift; each domain's unweighted per-entry mean squared error has multiplier one, coefficient squared norm penalty .001. Limit total physical length to 2. Offer scales .25, .5, 1 and KEEP. An otherwise identical label-shift control uses cyclically shifted query labels for the proposed update; it intentionally shares the correct-label basis. The no-write frozen-ridge condition participates throughout. Neither analytic candidate uses a VJP or BP. The subspace proposes simultaneous changes in the two full layers without exploring 2,048 independent directions in one solve.

## Strong same-parameter BP

Start at the identical two encoders, retaining head and new training data. Initialize a separate trainable target linear head from the full target training provisional ridge (.1) including its fixed initial feature normalization. Train every one of the 2,048 output-relevant coordinates through the exact recursive graph and this head with Adam, target cross entropy on logits multiplied by 5 and auxiliary Arabic squared centered old-score displacement (per entry). Search learning rates [.001,.005] crossed with old-loss weights [.25,2]; train each configuration for 64 steps, check steps 16 and 64. Each update uses 12 new and 12 old examples with replacement under seeded common data indices; accumulate in microbatches of 4, clip joint gradient norm to 2. Save the two encoder snapshots for each configuration. Each BP snapshot discards its provisional new head and receives the same fully refit ridge choice as every other candidate. Include KEEP. This is one bounded but tuned optimizer comparison; no method can use target test values in the search.

## Selection, verification and interpretation

With each candidate score every development example. Among snapshots whose old Arabic dev accuracy falls by at most .02 relative to the deep parent, choose highest AudioMNIST dev accuracy; tie by smaller new Brier, then smaller physical displacement, then stable order. Select sole frozen-ridge reference by highest new accuracy then Brier on the same grid. Seal the full changed weight vectors, both selected heads, bridge and calibration data provenance for all three parents and methods first, then newly launch cold scoring for every model. A failed fit or resource limit is recorded, never substituted with fabricated data. CPU time and wall time include setup, complete hyperparameter search, head refits and full cold validation. Separate measured original source acquisition, prepared-feature cost, and historical pretrained parent cost where known; the latter is not newly measured. Simultaneous CPU work may alter wall time.

Primary decision per parent: old deep parent must exceed .30 test accuracy, target accuracy gain over frozen-ridge >=.02, lower endpoint of 95% paired bootstrap across the 20 held target speakers strictly positive (10,000 participant resamples, fixed seed 28173), retained Arabic test loss at most .02, and target accuracy within .01 of tuned BP. Across all three require all per-parent criteria and >=10% reduction of aggregate charged fit+cold wall time for a matched-quality speed claim. Also report costs in CPU seconds and cohort-wide shared acquisition separately. Give every parent and control, target and retained accuracy/Brier and source-level uncertainty. Do not pool speakers across weight seeds as independent people. Face controls include wrong query labels, raw-feature ridge, and actual changes in each physical layer. No claim extends to large multimodal models.
