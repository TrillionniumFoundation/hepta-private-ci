# Supplemental board telemetry

Before training began, the desktop's qualified NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 reported driver 580.178.04 and a management limit of 115 W. The direct NVML `nvmlDeviceGetPowerUsage` and total-energy counter both returned `NVML_ERROR_NOT_SUPPORTED`. The reported power limit is a configured maximum. It does not measure consumption.

The same driver exposes actual power telemetry through `nvmlDeviceGetSamples` with `NVML_TOTAL_POWER_SAMPLES`. NVIDIA documents the sample timestamps as CPU time in microseconds and uses unsigned integer sample values for power (https://docs.nvidia.com/deploy/nvml-api/latest/api/group__nvmlDeviceQueries.html). The native diagnostic `nvidia-smi -q -d POWER` returned 119 recent measurements in 2.36 seconds, an average of 55.05 W at that observation. The recording process returned 119 valid samples in its first batch, with a last measured value of 53,362 mW at 1795820714282837 microseconds. These early measurements preceded any fit.

`observe_power_samples.py` persists deduplicated timestamp/power pairs as 12-byte little-endian records (`<QI`) in `board_observations/native_total_power.bin`. `observe_board.py` separately records board utilization, clocks, temperature, aggregate compute-context counts, the active training attempt and the filesystem time of each observed checkpoint. Its `board_w` field remains null on this device because the direct query is unsupported. Both streams use real clocks. The native stream is needed for power integration. Any integration should report the actual coverage, excluded gaps, observation-window choice and whether other applications shared the GPU. The initial activity sampler observed two pre-existing compute contexts outside the study. Board readings include desktop and other work during overlaps. The full program's electrical cost, process-specific electricity and facility energy require different instrumentation.
