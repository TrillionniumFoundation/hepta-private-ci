# Derivative-free local writes with the original diffusion drift

The original calibration gives drift 0.03355 in log distance. The finite-horizon first-hit probability decreases with the initial distance under a fixed wall. Coupling two paths with the same Brownian noise and different starting distances gives this ordering. In the live region the hitting density yields strict monotonicity. Because the wall increases with the bounded principal, local risk rises monotonically with its normalized drive up to absorption. An interior attainable probability therefore has a unique live normalized drive.

`drifted_edit.py` evaluates the two **unattainable limiting** tanh endpoints −1 and +1 only for bracketing. It rejects a target outside the open limiting probability interval, then bisects inside that interval and returns the finite preactivation, wall, principal, achieved probability and bracket. The method evaluates the actual analytic forward law and requires no backward pass. Its accuracy is set by an explicit probability tolerance. High or low saturation can produce much larger preactivation error than probability error; both are included in the verification. The zero-drift inverse in `microdomain.py` remains a distinct closed-form path.

The verification checked 372 local targets, with 31 drives for every combination of positive source drift, negative drift and zero drift and n in {1, 2, 4, 8}. All probability errors met 1e−12, the largest being 9.976e−13. For the original positive source drift, each of the four families converged within 41 bisection observations. `drifted_verification.json` includes the conditioning-sensitive drive errors and the unreachable-target rejection. The output of this 1-D inversion can be passed to the same frozen-key covariance solver as the zero-drift inverse. This result concerns a local fixed state. It does not propagate semantic supervision through a multilayer encoder.
