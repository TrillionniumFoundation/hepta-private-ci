## Frozen algorithms

Compare the author-code BetaEdit equation from Liu, Liu and Li (IJCAI 2026) with three ablations and two previously computed same-head controls. Author implementation at commit [8054c477](https://github.com/lbq8942/BetaEdit/blob/8054c4779638a8dd79770a20b16683d35ceb3383/BetaEdit/algs/betaedit/betaedit_main.py); see the [published paper](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2026/508) and [configuration](https://github.com/lbq8942/BetaEdit/blob/8054c4779638a8dd79770a20b16683d35ceb3383/BetaEdit/configs/algs/betaedit.yaml).

Use λ₁=3000, λ₂=10, threshold 0.02 for second-moment eigenvalues, projection refresh after at least 1,000 preceding requests, and reject a block with update Frobenius norm larger than 20 times the square root of its request count. Each stage visits all clips of the current sentence in stable ID order, in blocks of at most 32. In row convention, let C=AᵀA/200 for the 200 protected keys and G be the Gram sum of previous edit keys. Initial U consists of eigenvectors of C with eigenvalue below threshold. At a refresh take the low-eigenvalue basis of (G/n+3000C)/3001. Let k be the current keys and v their fixed parent-derived target vectors. The update is

    D = U [10I + Uᵀ(kᵀk + G + 3000C)U]⁻¹ Uᵀkᵀ(v−kW).

Check equivalence to the author's full projected normal system on an independent numerical fixture. Use FP64 for the solve and cast each applied updated head back to FP32. Record ranks, the actual refresh count and all update norms. Compare with the same equation under (a) frozen P with λ₁=3000, (b) refreshed P with the same protected subspace but no leakage term in its writing objective, and (c) frozen P with no leakage term. The latter is the AlphaEdit-style matrix rule on the same keys and targets. For all four every earlier Gram remains in the write objective. Contrast against the sealed μ=32 RLSEdit and 128-iteration downstream-cross-entropy Adam heads on the same parents, and the unchanged parents. The archived gradient arm reprocesses cumulative edit rows at every stage; exposure and optimization work differ by design. Its elapsed training cannot be inferred from loading its checkpoints. This panel measures accuracy and protection; it does not estimate equal-quality end-to-end acceleration.

## Endpoints

First compare each unscaled writer at all six stages. As a secondary deployment rule, apply a single scalar gain from 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1 per method to its parent-relative endpoint. Fit the scalar jointly over all 18 development seed/stage cells by largest number passing all gates, then median slack, minimum slack, then smaller gain. The next stage's writing state always follows the unscaled algorithm. The joint gate is current NLL gain ≥0.10, prior-edit NLL gain ≥0.05 where applicable, protected NLL increase ≤0.10 and protected top-1 loss ≤0.02. Seal all heads, development metrics and chosen scalar coefficients before scoring any held arrays in this experiment. Identify stage and all-seed joint passes on both development and held outcomes. Compare final current, past and protected NLLs via 4,000 held-actor resamples at fixed seeds. Independently reconstruct final logits in FP64. All head and unscaled final held-score files are retained with the original host experiment. Exact parent and control identities are enforced by the runnable program.
