# Dense and matrix-free killed transport on large uniform meshes

A T4 study after the 12/128-atom throughput and energy comparison. Use 256, 512, 1024 and 2048 atoms, each in float32 and float64. Four distinct normalized populations have moving boundaries, including instantaneous absorption in the negative-shift cases. The direct arm calls the published population kernel and explicitly contracts each destination matrix. The other arm calls the FFT equivalent on the same mesh, same fixed drift, volatility and horizon. Both recompute both He–Xiong monetary channels from the current principal and grid. A common scalar loss combines first-passage flow, equity, monetary shortage, a smooth probe and remaining mass energy. Save all resulting distributions, readouts and gradients with respect to initial mass, boundary shift and principal. Compare the scaled maximum absolute error, defined as maximum absolute difference divided by one plus the reference's largest magnitude. Also test live plus absorbed mass against one. Prespecified tolerances are 2e-9 in float64 and 3e-4 in float32. Only matched numerics qualify a performance ratio.

A second pair at 256 and 1024 atoms passes three frames through a real two-layer width-two HCSN in double precision. Save all terminal states, outputs and the reverse derivatives of the loss for all trainable parameters and all input frames. This uses a randomly initialized small network to assess hardware and arithmetic scaling.

Use one private Colab T4, with observed CUDA device and arithmetic. A fresh process isolates each arm and limits the PyTorch allocator to 75% of its visible device capacity. Retain OOM and unrelated errors separately. After three warm-ups estimate enough calls for a two-second window, bounded to 3–384 complete calls. Every timed call synchronizes the device and includes forward and reverse differentiation. Record all synchronized wall times and CUDA event intervals, live and peak allocated bytes and raw board counter energy divided by completed calls. Record contemporaneous 5-Hz NVML power. The workload combines small-budget kernel launches and Python overhead. This test measures warmed calls; the preceding 12/128 study measured cold time to a task-quality threshold. Independent compressed raw numerical arrays accompany the JSON telemetry. A single observation per arm is insufficient to resolve marginal differences at a precise crossover point.
