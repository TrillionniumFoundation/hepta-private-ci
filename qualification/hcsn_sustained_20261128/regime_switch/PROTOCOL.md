# One-way Markov liquidity regime and an endogenous continuation wall

The original paper derives the boundary and equity for a stationary contract. Test a genuinely forward-looking alternative to recomputing a stationary wall at every observation. Fix the original rounded BB debt and asset coefficients. The current liquidity premium is l0. At a risk-neutral exponentially distributed time of intensity h, independent of the diffusion, it changes permanently to l1. A neural layer may later condition h on present information; no future input is used in this solver. After the transition use the published one-state equity, debt of the correct remaining maturity, and endogenous B1. If the regime jumps when V≤B1, the bond recovers alpha*V/m immediately and equity becomes zero. Keep notional and coupon schedules constant across the macro state. Mu and sigma are unchanged. This macro intensity is distinct from the trading cost in the paper.

Compute the pre-regime endogenous B0 by value matching and smooth fit with the true switching debt in the continuation cash flow, using the killed Gaussian propagation and the source from the post-state. Test transitions from .01 to .05, from .05 to .01, and from .01 to itself at h=0,.1,1,4 per unit time. Compute the post-state separately in each panel. Numerical competition uses a distinct finite-difference diffusion/discount/default bond with maturity evolution and a separate elliptic equity boundary-value solver. Compare fitted B0, original no-switch limits, identical-state invariance, nonnegative continuation equity, stop-region continuation residual, recovery jump strips, equity and debt at selected V. No universal convergence of B0 at infinite intensity is assumed. Include the neural use of the endogenous boundary under an input-dependent current hazard and its implicit derivative at regular roots. Report computational cost relative to the closed stationary wall and distinguish the exact integral formulation from numerical quadrature, finite differences and the stationary price suboperators. These results concern this specified one-way model. Prior research already considers macroeconomic states in credit models.
