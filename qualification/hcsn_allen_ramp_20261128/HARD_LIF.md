# Fully autonomous hard-threshold LIF as an onset sensitivity

The registered point-process LIF predicts a physical escape intensity from its leaky membrane and conditions state reset on actually observed events. For a second test, integrate an explicit deterministic leaky membrane from the recorded current, fire when its own membrane crosses threshold, reset it to zero and insert one silent bin. Its state never reads measured spikes. No cell from the held cohort has entered fitting or selection of this supplementary model.

Use the same 2-ms bins, sample splits and observed censoring. A membrane time constant of 5, 15 or 40 ms and a current-equivalent threshold from 20, 40, 60, 80, 100, 130, 160, 200, 250, 320, 400, 600 and 800 pA generate candidate deterministic sequences. Apply a 6-ms causal exponential kernel of unit initial height to its own events. To give a finite probability for noisy observed timing, fit a nonnegative baseline and positive kernel gain by specimen-balanced Bernoulli maximum likelihood on training, with event probability one minus the exponential of negative baseline-plus-gain per bin. This observation model does not change the autonomous dynamics. Select the candidate with lowest development NLL, refit those two parameters on train and development, and seal it before held scoring. Keep its raw deterministic spikes plus probabilities for every held sweep. Report observed versus directly predicted first spikes, first-or-censor NLL, timing absolute error with silence assigned the observation end, the numbers of false positive and missing onset predictions, and its paired first-or-censor score difference from the registered dual-price predictor. Include examples where silence is badly predicted.

During training-only estimation the first double-precision L-BFGS-B solver returned an abnormal line-search termination before producing a seal. The equivalent silent-bin objective now uses the clipped cumulative hazard directly, the line-search allowance is 80 and a deterministic bounded Powell retry applies only on convergence failure. Each candidate records the solver, final objective and iterations. The candidate grid, parameter bounds, selection criterion, data and posterior scoring formulas are identical. Both models must still be independently sealed before any held score.
