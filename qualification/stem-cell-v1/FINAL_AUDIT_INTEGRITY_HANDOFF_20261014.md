## Two actual defects corrected

The prior campaign cost projection checked numerical totals against scope sums but
did not reject a numerical complete total when those scopes themselves contained
unknown operators or failed calls. A recomputed outer hash can bind contradictory
content; hashing is not a substitute for checking that content.

`work_record.validate_work_record` now runs both in the normal sealed export and
in final-campaign cost projection, including failed attempts. It checks bounded
scope/operator inventories, integer types, duplicate scopes, attempted/failed
counts, uncertainty and aborted reservations. A numerical complete phase count
requires at least one observed scope, no unknown or failed scope in that phase,
and equality with the known executed prefix. Unobserved or incomplete phases
remain null, even when their currently known prefix happens to be zero. Existing
formula units, profiler schemas, execution algorithms and scientific gates are
not relaxed or upgraded.

Formula uncertainty and failed execution are separate observations. One unknown
operator that also throws can legitimately increment scope uncertainty twice.
A real Long-tensor in-place sine failure exposed an initially overstrict check;
the corrected validator retains both events and the valid failure record, but
still rejects any claimed complete total. This failed development reproduction
is retained alongside the passing regression, not rewritten as a pass.

The prior WorkLedger report also returned the live policy and scope dictionaries.
A consumer editing its report could therefore change the internal ledger and
subsequent reports. Reports now deep-copy their nested contents. A consumer may
edit its own copy, but cannot mutate the live ledger through those references.
A later stop does not rewrite an earlier report. This is object-ownership hygiene,
not protection against arbitrary code directly accessing internal Python state.
