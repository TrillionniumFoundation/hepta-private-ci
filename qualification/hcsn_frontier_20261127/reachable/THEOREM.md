# Common-reset reachable states and the limits of a priced message

Fix the grid, time step, reset interpolation and coefficients of qualification/hcsn_continuous_20261127/core, with either eight or twelve active atoms and one refractory state. Take one sender whose external drive has weight one, bias zero and whose recurrent and dual-price coefficients are zero. This is an allowed set of network weights. Levels are log boundary relative to the nominal boundary. Since the nominal principal is inside its tanh range and the boundary's principal coefficient is positive, every sufficiently small level, of either sign, is realized by a finite input.

**Claim.** There are two finite input histories from the same reset, ending at the same nominal boundary, with identical current flow, equity and monetary loss, and distinct flow after one further identical zero input. The claim also holds when the receiver additionally sees the four default horizon probes. In this neighborhood, any linear time-local message sufficient for all future nominal-input flows must injectively encode the active state on the mass-one simplex.

**Argument.** Write the full probability vector as a row with A active masses and a final refractory mass. M(d) is its (A+1)-square transition when last level minus current level equals d. Use the difference of the free and reflected normal interval probabilities for active destinations, its complement for first hit and the previous refractory row for reset. At zero all live atoms have strictly positive margin. The mathematical kernel is analytic in a neighborhood of zero; every live-bin kernel is positive there. Set M=M(0) and D=M'(0). Start from row p given by the two-point reset interpolation. Run A+1 steps with A freely chosen levels, and the last level fixed at zero. At the all-zero history the j-th derivative of the terminal row is

    p M^(j-1) (M D - D M) M^(A-j),     j=1,...,A.

The first term accounts for the effect of the j-th level on step j+1, the second for its opposite effect on step j. The omitted terminal probability coordinate is determined by total mass one. The determinant in rank.json for the resulting square active-coordinate Jacobian excludes zero. The inverse-function theorem therefore gives an open subset of reachable terminal states in the simplex.

At a fixed terminal boundary all currently reported prices and probes are linear functionals of the terminal active masses. Current flow is the last state coordinate. Let f be its coordinate column. The next flow under a common zero input is the linear functional M f. For each port, the reported positive Gram determinant for the columns consisting of total mass, port observations and M f proves that M f is outside their span. Inside the open reachable neighborhood move in a null direction of the observed columns on which M f changes. Small opposite perturbations yield the two histories claimed. The matrix with columns one, f, M f, ..., M^(A-1)f has nonzero determinant; its nominal-input flow sequence identifies the full probability state. Consequently, any linear message sufficient for that future must have rank A on the simplex tangent. The evidence treats the original finite-grid equations over real arithmetic; floating-point replays give separate numeric tolerances. These findings concern one present message. A receiver that maintains previous observations has additional information.
