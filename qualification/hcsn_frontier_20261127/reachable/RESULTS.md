# Reachability and compression results

On the specified eight-atom grid the worst difference in the contemporary price and flow coordinates of the two replays is 5.94e-13, while the next flow differs by 0.00667522. On twelve atoms the respective values are 6.81e-11 and 0.00574438. Adding four physical horizon channels reduces the size of the example differences: the replays produced next-step gaps of 3.40e-12 and 9.23e-9 on eight and twelve atoms. The existence of exact collisions derives from the interval proof and inverse function theorem. The replay reports finite-precision differences and includes all search attempts.

For 800 reachable twelve-atom states driven by a previously unseen persistent-switching policy, paired with 96 fresh future schedules drawn from the same policy and four endpoints per schedule, the three priced outputs yield probability RMSE 1.057e-3. The seven priced-plus-horizon outputs yield 4.132e-6. A seven-coordinate message selected from a nominal future-flow dictionary yields 9.365e-7, versus 1.787e-4 for seven principal components of the states. At width three, the priced message outperforms the future dictionary on this pair of shifted policies. At width eleven the future dictionary yields 1.872e-10. The full state reconstructs within double-precision roundoff. The error is over model-generated current-state and future-schedule pairs, not biological events or learned multimodal tasks. Source and output retain all four distribution combinations and per-horizon and worst-case errors.

For an individual, fixed future schedule and a single endpoint, the sender can transmit the exact scalar projection of the present state onto that schedule's backward first-hit vector. With an unknown arbitrary future, no fixed one-scalar linear port can reproduce every future flow on this reachable simplex. It is therefore meaningful to set a predictive error budget or to condition ports on receiver queries.
