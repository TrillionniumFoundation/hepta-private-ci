# Do barriers anticipate a change of regime?

Static He–Xiong prices are exact under the paper's stationary environment. A network that moves between stationary boundaries after each input has made an additional modeling assumption. Investigate the tractable alternative in which the cost is modulated by an exogenous two-state continuous-time Markov chain and the stopping rule anticipates future switches. To isolate this effect, the running continuation payoff is payout times value minus the current regime cost. This reduced neural cost model discards finite-maturity debt renewal. Compare with its own frozen-environment solution, never with an alleged exact mark for the full debt model.

Use financial coefficients r=.08, payout=.02, volatility=.23, costs .04/.08 and public baseline transition intensities .12/.16 per model time unit. Use unit recovery on first hit discounted at .09 to form a second output. Compute the two endogenous boundaries by value matching and smooth pasting for continuation, then fit the transforms to those boundaries with their proper boundary conditions. Verify against independent finite-difference obstacle and Dirichlet solvers, including the switch-into-stop channel. Preserve the model equations and errors, check interior residuals, ordering, superharmonicity in the stopped region and convergence under independent spatial refinement. Use finite differences to check implicit sensitivities to each cost and transition intensity. Evaluate slow and fast switching for their mathematical limits. Treat the switching-time jump and any new reset as explicit neural design choices. No human cohort or held-out classification data will be accessed by this diagnostic.

Regime-switching smooth-fit problems predate this repository. See Q. Zhang and X. Guo, Closed-Form Solutions for Perpetual American Put Options with Regime Switching, DOI 10.1137/S0036139903426083.
