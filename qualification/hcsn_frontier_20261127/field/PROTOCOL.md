# Observation costs and first exhaustion on metered electricity traces

This prospective comparison uses measured demand and explicitly constructed contracts. No actual household bill, intervention, failure or credit default is imputed. The label is the first exhaustion of a declared reserve when debited by future measured consumption and a known scheduled fee. This tests learning a cost-dependent event from real sequences. It allows a simple non-neural conditional historical baseline to win. Inputs and labels are generated without the He–Xiong pricing formula.

## Data and endpoints

Use UCI 235 (Sceaux, France) raw minute-level active power, and UCI 374 (Belgium) ten-minute appliance and lighting energy. Aggregate complete half-hour intervals to kWh, allowing no missing subintervals. Use anchors every two hours on the recorded local clock; require a complete 48-hour history and three-hour future. Divide energy by the mean of the previous 96 half-hour slots. Only the most recent 24 half-hours enter the learned sequence. Use clock sine/cosine and past load. Reserve levels are 2.5 and 5 typical half-hour expenditures. Two matched contracts have fee 0 or 1.25 at future slot 3. Electricity multiplier is 1.55 for future half-hours beginning at 17:00–20:59, otherwise 1. Targets are cumulative expenditure including the fee greater than or equal to the reserve by 2, 4 or 6 half-hour slots. All arms get identical tariff, reserve and fee information.

Train before 2009-07-01, select on 2009-07-01 through 2009-12-31, test 2010 rows in France. Require full history and outcome to belong to each split. All eligible Belgian rows form a separate external test, with no refitting. Only train and development inputs and labels may enter model or checkpoint selection. Cold scoring follows one seal for the complete selected matrix. All paired contracts share anchor and split. Report equal-anchor mean Brier and cost-response error (absolute discrepancy between mean predicted fee effect and mean empirical paired fee effect, for the 2h and 3h endpoints). Resample entire contiguous calendar weeks jointly within source for descriptive intervals.

## Algorithms, budget and falsifiers

Four recurrent/sequence models: a He–Xiong expected-reset mean cell with price feedback and a cost-driven principal, its locally slope-matched smooth replacement with the same principal, reset and parameter count, GRU, and a shallow causal attention encoder. Fixed controls may differ in parameter count; disclose counts, hardware, input features, precision and all optimizers. Common AdamW, width 24, one temporal block, batch 96, seeds 173/331/509/773. Compare with empirical nearest training-day/load histories using k from 16,64,256, chosen on French development Brier. Use expanding CPU training budgets 30,120,600,2520 seconds per arm/seed, single-thread and one candidate training process at a time. The full 16 fits therefore entail 11.2 hours of active single-core optimization before validation, cold evaluation and bootstrap. Record process wall, preparation, optimizer, validation, checkpoints and cold cost separately; checkpoint transitions must preserve optimizer and sampler. Lower/upper limits for time to quality come from the sampled checkpoints. Reference targets on development are best kNN development Brier and 0.98 times that number. Equal budget is not equal quality.

Primary hypothesis requires lower Brier for the literal law versus slope-matched control on *both* test sources; give paired week intervals. Disclose direction against every baseline. A joint lower error and time-to-quality advantage over the strongest eligible baseline is a separate criterion. Report distinct learning and causal-response results, even if their directions disagree. Energy/Joules and general foundation-model scaling are outside this CPU experiment. These costs are exogenous for the constructed benchmark; the cell's structural barrier is computed from principal conditional on its inputs. The experiment is not a fit of endogenous household finance.
