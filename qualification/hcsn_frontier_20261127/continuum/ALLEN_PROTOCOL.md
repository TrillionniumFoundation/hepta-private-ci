# Count-current curves implied by a continuously regenerating drift neuron

Reuse two different already-archived Allen Cell Types evaluations from qualification/hcsn_research_20261127/feedback/ephys (record archives ephys_records.zip and nwb_curve_records.zip). These are retrospective comparisons on published within-cell held-out current amplitudes. The first archive excludes missing-count sweeps, whereas the NWB cohort includes genuine zero responses. Fit each cell separately to its archived training currents and counts. Do not tune hyperparameters with the previously reported test results.

For drive-normalized current z fit the analytic equilibrium model r(z)=max(z−theta,0)/(a+d max(z−theta,0)), a>0, d≥0. Units absorb input scaling; equilibrium intensity is inferred under a negative physical log-margin drift linear beyond rheobase. This is identical to a generic rectified saturating response. A match does not identify a He–Xiong neural mechanism. Use multistart bounded ordinary least squares with fixed initial conditions for (theta,a,d); report flat and near-linear limits and preserve all parameters. Predict expected counts in approximately one-second long-square sweeps. Training durations are approximated as one second because the first compressed fit archive omits them; use individually recorded test duration when supplied. Compare against the original sealed LIF, rectifier, interpolation, softplus, QVI and constant predictions. Primary score mean per-cell held-out absolute count error; paired resampling across cells, not current levels. Keep the NWB and summary-derived cohorts distinct, disclose overlapping specimen IDs, and report zeros vs spiking levels in the NWB set. This finite-sweep test also measures error from using the asymptotic rate rather than the transient count.
