# The native cell detects boundary changes but does not encode a stationary current in its steady firing rate

At any constant log boundary b, consecutive log boundaries are equal. Their difference, the only location shift used in the killed transition, is therefore zero. In the original moving grid, the reset is specified at a fixed distance to that boundary. The state update at every subsequent tick is M(0), independent of b. This finite chain is irreducible and aperiodic: strictly positive active-to-active entries, positive first-hit probability, and a return from refractory to the reset support. It has one invariant distribution. At any equilibrium with constant barrier the firing rate is its refractory coordinate, independent of the level of that barrier. Prices at a given margin depend separately on principal and can still transmit steady information. Internal feedback may prevent settling to a constant barrier; the argument makes no claim about such oscillating or input-driven regimes.

At twelve native atoms, one boundary displacement of −0.16 or +0.16 gives next firing mass 0.00057121 or 0.04446500 from the same nominal equilibrium. Both approach 0.00864335 per tick after the boundary is held constant. At eight atoms the common equilibrium is 0.01040808 per tick. This difference between transient and steady effects is important for step-current experiments.

In an unbounded domain the published log drift is +0.03355, the reset margin is 0.30, and the volatility is 0.23. The probability of ever hitting the lower wall from reset is exp(−2 mu x/sigma²)=0.68349954. Reset applies only after a hit. Repeated independent trials ending in escape yield a geometric number of hits with mean p/(1−p)=2.15955304 and finite total count almost surely. Consequently the asymptotic rate vanishes. In the finite model, redistributing all of the top bin to its finite representative prevents permanent escape. When the top is moved from 1.3 to 6.5 at similar spacing, computed stationary intensity per unit time falls from 0.03209 to 0.00003899. The script independently evaluates the total-count reference over finite horizons.

A constructive correction holds the He–Xiong price equations fixed for the economic channels and supplies a separate physical drift to the first-passage neuron. With constant negative physical drift, the one-cycle hitting time is inverse Gaussian, has mean x/|mu|, and hits almost surely. With fixed refractory delay d the renewal rate is 1/(d+x/|mu|). At zero drift passage is almost sure with infinite mean time, producing zero limiting rate. Positive drift produces terminal escape almost surely after finitely many spikes. An input-dependent drift that changes sign therefore gives the analytic model a genuine tonic threshold. For a linear drift across rheobase the frequency-current relation is a rectified, saturating rational function. It is not unique to a financial construction. The readout of monetary expected loss continues to use the original pricing law; the firing distribution uses the stated physical dynamics. This extension changes an explicit modeling assumption.
