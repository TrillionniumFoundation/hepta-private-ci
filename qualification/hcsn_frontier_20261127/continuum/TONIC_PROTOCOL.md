# Constant-input firing and the limit at long times

The question is whether changing only the equilibrium He–Xiong boundary can create a steady firing-rate response to constant drive in the supplied moving-grid cell. At a fixed barrier, the margin shift equals zero, so evaluate the transition matrix and its invariant probability. Compare stationary flow under eleven constant log barriers including nominal, both grid sizes 8 and 12; separately vary the upper numerical domain at approximately constant spacing. Distinguish transient flow, equilibrium flow, and possible time-dependent attractors in learned feedback. In the unbounded diffusion with the original positive drift, calculate the defective probability of ever hitting the barrier, the expected number of regenerative spikes before escape, and the limiting steady rate. Do not infer physiological tonic firing from repeated events in a finite trace.

As a constructive extension, decouple the physical log-margin drift (actual firing law) from the risk-neutral price coefficients and permit constant drive to set a negative drift. The absorbing density and first-hit formula remain analytic and the finite-mean renewal rate can be computed exactly. Demonstrate rate vs input, compare the finite-horizon expected count per time to the renewal limit, and check the gate for nonpositive vs positive drift. No observation is used to select the sign. This is a model design and idealized numeric validation; biological inference and downstream performance are independent.
