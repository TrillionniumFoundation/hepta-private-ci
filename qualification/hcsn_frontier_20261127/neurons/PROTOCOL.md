# Predicting recorded neuronal spikes from imposed currents

Use the public CRCNS ch-epfl-2009 challenges A (regular spiking), B (fast spiking) and C (somatic and dendritic drive). Each recording is one cell. Count positive-going somatic 0 mV crossings in 2 ms bins from the available 10 kHz traces. All measurements use causal current up to the end of the previous bin, with no measured voltage or prior recorded spike as an input; each arm thus predicts firing for a fresh repeat of the imposed stimulus. Independent labels are aggregated only after detecting spikes within each recorded trial. Use the archive's publicly labelled portion, at most the first 38 seconds, avoiding its undisclosed official test labels. Split by distinct aligned stimulus time for every repeat jointly. The first 26 seconds are for fitting, 26–31 seconds for selection and 31–38 seconds for cold evaluation. A fixed 200 ms washout at each boundary is omitted from scoring. Models may condition on earlier currents, including pre-split history, never on their future. Each cell is independently fitted; results have three-cell descriptive scope.

**Comparison.** A twelve-atom first-passage population with exact He–Xiong boundary and two priced feedback channels, single-step refractory reset, driven by a standardized causal exponential bank of the applied current. Include an identically wired variant replacing price channels with locally slope-matched smooth functions of margin at the nominal principal (preserve coefficients' principal dependence), a first-passage model with feedback disabled, a stochastic leaky-integrate-and-fire baseline with membrane leak, escape and absolute refractory, a regularized temporal logistic GLM built from the same input and a constant firing-rate baseline.
