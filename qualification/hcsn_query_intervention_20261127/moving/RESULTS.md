# Exact crossing of a moving boundary and its parameter ambiguity

On one time interval let the latent log state start at a with drift μ and diffusion σ>0, and let the log boundary be b+s t. A constant-speed wall is exactly tractable. In the relative coordinate the initial distance is x=a−b and drift is μ−s. With u=x/σ, v=(μ−s)/σ and t>0, the hitting probability before t is

F = Φ(−u/√t−v√t)+exp(−2uv) Φ(−u/√t+v√t).

For a discount q>0 and w=√(v²+2q), the discounted hitting mass is exp[(w−v)u] F(u,w,t). This follows by multiplying the first-hitting density u exp[−(u+vt)²/(2t)]/√(2πt³) by exp(−qt). A query sender therefore has an exact single-segment probability and discounted loss operator even when the barrier moves at a constant speed in log coordinates. Concatenation requires the surviving endpoint distribution; individual segment hitting probabilities generally cannot be multiplied.

At x=0.15, σ=.23, μ=.03355 and horizon one, changing the boundary slope from −.18 through zero to +.18 yields hitting probabilities .238342, .465671 and .720346. Freezing the beginning of the wall commands .465671 in all three cases. Replacing it by its final absolute level commands .121931, .465671 and 1.000000. Across 15 settings, independent Brownian-bridge simulations of 350,000 paths per setting differed from the moving-wall law by at most 2.2501 standard errors. Quadrature of the killed-density survivor mass plus the analytic hit mass differed from one by at most 9.4e−15.

## What an end-to-end gradient can learn

Write I=exp(−2uv) Φ(−u/√t+v√t) and A=−u/√t−v√t. On x>0 the partials are F_u=−2φ(A)/√t−2vI and F_v=−2uI. This gives analytic derivatives with respect to all five supplied parameters. The maximum discrepancy against two-sided finite differences was 1.46e−8 over all fifteen event and discounted-event outputs. At contact, the absorbing extension to x≤0 has left derivative zero, while F's continuation-side derivative was −5.923196 in the specified .25-time case. This distinction matters if outputs are built directly from a point state; the stationary smooth-pasting condition on equity applies to a different quantity.

For any number of horizons and discounts the entire first-hit and discounted-first-hit observations depend on the two dimensionless combinations u,v. Thus the five-dimensional parameter vector (a,b,μ,s,log σ) gives an event-observation Jacobian of rank at most two. It reached rank two numerically, with first singular values 14.4725 and 1.8732 and the remaining three below 3e−16. A joint translation of a,b, a joint change to μ,s, and a positive rescaling of x,μ−s,σ left all 15 values unchanged to ≤2.3e−16. More generally adding the same deterministic time-varying trajectory to a latent log state and its boundary preserves all sample-path hitting times. It changes both their absolute trajectories. This is an exact identifiability obstruction for training from first-hit data alone, independent of the optimizer.

For this five-parameter example an independent measurement of the absolute initial state, a separate unconditional mean of the absolute state at t=.5 and one independent increment-variance-rate measurement raise the observed local ranks successively to 3, 4 and 5. These are illustrative idealized measurements and their noise was not modeled. In a neural implementation one may instead train the two event coordinates directly; separately interpreting learned absolute boundary location or its speed requires constraints or independent signal. Additional constant-discount put-style readouts of the same event law do not supply the missing information. An independently observed equity channel with a specified numerical scale changes the observation map and needs its own rank check.
