# Stationary mean flux, engineered feedback pulses and local training stability

At constant input, an active reserve bin with positive diffusion has positive probability of surviving to any finite interior bin and positive probability of default. The refractory row releases into a positive active reset distribution. Thus the finite transition matrix squared is strictly positive: even a refractory start can release into an active state, then reach any active bin or the next refractory state. The matrix is primitive, giving a unique stationary distribution and convergence from every initial population. A single stochastic particle can repeatedly spike under these assumptions; the deterministic population mean cannot sustain a modulated periodic flux at constant input. In the actual 12+1 model the second-largest eigenvalue modulus was .954393, stationary flux .00864335 per update and stationary balance residual 4.78e−17. The smallest numerical two-step entry was 1.79e−15.

Adding fast positive and slow negative activity feedback to the command changes the populations' dynamical behavior. In 132 prespecified gain/filter combinations, none passed the strict integer-orbit return rule. Several displayed large flux oscillations over the final 512 steps. The preregistered follow-up tested three previously unexamined gains, 80/96/112, alongside 64 and 128 and an open-loop control. Over 8,192 steps and both signs of a physical initial perturbation, all 15 nonzero-gain cases (five gains × three wall laws) passed the stated peak-regularity and tail-stability criteria. All three open-loop controls failed that criterion, as expected; none showed regular pulses. No long trace met the exact integer-return rule. Thus the data distinguish regular pulsing from a certified mathematical limit cycle.

At gain 96, slow negative gain 144 and smoothing .05, mean inter-peak intervals in model bins were 105.553 fixed, 99.350 tangent and 94.262 literal; their interpeak coefficients of variation were .00471, .00480 and .00466. Peak-to-peak flux excursions were .08232, .08138 and .07992. The literal law produced regular pulses over the held gains; the matched controls did as well. There is no observed categorical pulse-generation ability unique to the finance boundary. The model time and gains are synthetic and unconstrained by measured physiology.

At the common equilibrium the literal and tangent scalar boundaries have equal level and first derivative. Composing either smooth wall with the same update then gives the same tangent-space Jacobian by the chain rule. Their maximum difference across the tested finite-difference Jacobians was 5.19e−8. Nonlinear waveform differences can therefore coexist with the same onset of local instability. The largest measured spectral radius was 1.37951 and the maximum 32-step Jacobian spectral norm about 1.89 million; the latter is coordinate-dependent. A differentiable recurrence hence permits gradient calculation while long-horizon gradients near an unstable equilibrium can be large. Single-step Jacobians computed at two finite-difference resolutions differed by ≤2.15e−6.
