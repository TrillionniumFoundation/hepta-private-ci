# Direct two-layer speech edits reach the retained-speaker quality target

The first frozen experiment supports actual changes to both hidden layers of a trained HCSN speech classifier through a forward-in-time sensitivity solve. It does not establish a training-time advantage over BP or pricing-specific superiority. On the earliest rung, all three literal parents preserved the preregistered old-domain accuracy floor and gained at least two percentage points on each of two separately held-out AudioMNIST speaker panels. End-to-end Adam on the same two matrices reached the same gate on the same rung. An ordinary additive acoustic ridge was the fastest successful comparison and yielded the best new-domain scores.

All candidate files were committed in SEAL.json at 85516cfd32ec05385459a30845dde549fc67ac81 before the independent inference process began. The archive at 70b43e565f77e8809ae8d317312a86a3db24361e holds the 237 exact candidate, parent and fit-record files. The nine fitted procedures have three nested choices for each of six parents, 162 choices in total. Several choices share a weight state. The separate unedited parent is also independently evaluated. Inference reconstructed the two recurrent layers from the field table using a separate bilinear implementation, preserving independent logits for old, A, B and C.

Accuracy first averages recordings within speaker, then speakers within each frozen parent, then three parent initializations. The old task has 300 utterances from six original FSDD speakers. A and B each have 1,040 recordings from 26 separate AudioMNIST speakers. C consists of 240 genuinely third-source Speech Commands recordings across 118 speakers. Effective sample size for the confidence intervals is the number of speakers. Intervals resample the same speaker for both algorithms and hold the three parents fixed. They are pointwise and cannot establish population-level superiority over random initializations.

At three CPU seconds the paired 95% intervals for the full forward-sensitivity gain over the corresponding unedited literal parents were +11.19 to +15.71 percentage points on A and +10.10 to +17.24 on B. Against same-position Adam the corresponding differences were −0.10 to +2.60 and −0.03 to +2.47 points. Both intervals satisfy the two-point quality noninferiority margin. The matched cold time ratio was 1.049 versus Adam and 0.970 versus L-BFGS. Both fail the preregistered ratio ceiling of 0.80. The first qualifying secant solutions took 2.542 times the matched Adam time. Dev-selected performance at a larger budget can lose old accuracy. At 60 seconds Adam preserved the old criterion for one of three parents.

For all three literal parents the selected forward-sensitivity updates to the 16×12 sender and 12×12 receiver at three seconds had numerical rank 12 each. Frobenius changes for the sender/receiver were 2.253/2.050, 2.237/1.719 and 2.390/1.747. Independently evaluating 32 real support utterances at the unchanged parents reproduced logits to within 8.9e−16 across six fits. The observed logit response had rank 288 for 336 writable coefficients. A 1e−6 finite displacement yielded a maximum local directional discrepancy of about 0.001 in the most sensitive literal seed. Piecewise interpolation can cross grid knots. The method explicitly uses targets and downstream logit sensitivities; a finite ridge subproblem remains embedded in a nonlinear iterative algorithm.

C did not meet the preregistered three-parent confirmation rule for any literal method. The literal full-sensitivity gain at three seconds averaged +2.95 points, with a speaker-paired interval of −0.73 to +6.73. The matched smooth model passed the third-domain gate at some later rungs, including 60 seconds for two-layer secants and full sensitivity. The smooth full-sensitivity A/B accuracies at 60 seconds were 85.90%/80.42%, versus 85.06%/79.62% for literal. Comparisons with a matched smooth curve and an ordinary ridge therefore constrain architecture-specific claims.

The acoustic ridge needed 11.957 s versus 18.015 s for matched Adam from already available WAV files. The common cost includes waveform preparation, parent construction, study preprocessing, matched validation search and cold evaluation of old/A/B. It excludes preceding network fetches, and the third-source acquisition sits outside the primary timing comparison. Adding more than 12.275 unmeasured common seconds eliminates the 20% ridge advantage. Shared CPU load affects elapsed ratios, and no accelerator training or energy advantage was measured. The evidence distinguishes a direct two-site edit of a pretrained network from the separate ease of linear feature adaptation. A claim of general BP replacement remains open.
