# Corpus scale and training curves for complete raw dual encoders

Registered before opening the previous screen's selected and aggregated cold results and before reading any model score on the additional datasets. The first study trained under 512 updates on one author corpus. This second study increases the training horizon, data diversity and model capacity, adds a hard-reset LIF control, and tests an additional geographic corpus. Raw inputs only. It asks whether a small signed boundary-state population generalizes and improves time-to-quality as the encoder receives more realistic optimization.

Training uses filtered RSICD official train and NWPU-captions official train. Source development is the two official validation splits. Source tests are the corresponding official test splits. External UCM is a repeated comparison, and the designated new external gallery is all Sydney-Captions images with nonempty primary human captions. Collect pinned raw data, record complete file SHA, and screen for exact RGB and transformed 64-bit perceptual hash duplicates against accepted source train/dev and within each evaluation gallery (Hamming <=4). Do not tune against external targets. Report group counts, losses and overlaps. Use exclusively the training captions for word IDs, capped at 4096, with max 24 words. Images 32×32 raw RGB. Each device clock is explicit; the primary resource is one pinned CPU thread with FP32 and fixed open-source library versions.

All raw inputs and the full two-modality encoder train jointly from random initialization. Use width 96, three stacked residual temporal layers, four groups with 4 live atoms and one refractory state, 32-D readout, and same image/text frontend across four arms: literal continuation/loss + endogenous boundary + new first-passage mass, matched smooth prices with the same kernel, surrogate-gradient hard LIF, and parameter-comparable scaled dot-product attention. Apply contrastive symmetric caption-group-aware cross entropy with temperature 1/15, balanced source sampling (16 RSICD/16 NWPU examples per 32-example batch), deterministic choice among five captions and seeds 2039, 4729 and 8047. AdamW .001, decay .01, gradient norm clipped at one. All arms use identical ordered mini-batches under the same seed.

Use single-thread process CPU budgets 30, 120, 600, 2100 and 4200 seconds per independent arm/seed. Measure the step onset and completion crossing each budget, complete indivisible minibatches, and store all parameters, optimizer, CPU RNG, NumPy sampler, step, accumulated training CPU/wall and validation duration. Test discrete equal-update states at 128, 512, 2048, 8192, 32768 and 65536 if reached. Save source-development choices at all registered observations and report overshoot and actual CPU/elapsed times. Cold process start and input preparation carry separate costs. All 4×3 final runs and every eligible endpoint must be sealed before new external scoring. Deterministic interruptions resume the same state/sampler and accrued training; system downtime is not training time and is measured separately. Periodic restart artifacts may be created more often for recovery.

Primary preregistered outcome: photo-weighted mean bidirectional pair concordance on Sydney, excluding identical normalized primary captions, at the highest source-selected 4200-second candidate. To establish superiority the literal arm must exceed both matched smooth and SDPA with positive lower 95% bounds from 2000 paired caption-cluster resamples and the mismatched control must be within .05 of .5. Report LIF, well-tuned source-selected linear CCA, R@1, matching with one modality fixed, and lexical-hard negative concordance independently. For equal-quality speed test use matched gates .60, .65, .70; all three seeds must independently achieve the joint gate on both source tests and Sydney while the shuffled control is within .05 of chance. Count measured end-to-end startup/data/selection/inference costs and also give first-fit numbers with reusable input caches. Preserve missed gates. GPU/energy, other modalities and much larger-scale training require distinct evidence.
