# Directed boundary-wire and channel interventions in the trained raw encoder

The original UCM results and weight hashes are known. This is a secondary, frozen-weight mechanistic test, registered before running these interventions. Keep each of three literal encoders at its source-only selected weights. Measure exact same rows and two-direction retrieval concordance on both prior RSICD test and UCM. No optimization, additional selection or new Sydney observation is allowed.

Compare factual replay with the following five interventions: zero only the lower-to-upper boundary projection input, substitute upper-layer wire inputs from different same-modality photos using a deterministic caption-group derangement, suppress the E channel at both layers, suppress the loss channel at both layers, and suppress both channels. The last three modifications remove the selected channel from readout and later recurrent price feedback. Lower-layer hidden residuals and all direct image/text inputs remain available. Evaluate a sham true-wire substitution from recorded first-layer marks, requiring the same predicted vectors as direct factual replay. Use explicit equality checks for gallery IDs and factual vectors against the immutable cold deposits.

For each seed/condition/gallery report mean feature-vector displacement, paired concordance, exact and equivalence Recall@1 and signed concordance change. For UCM derive 2,000 normalized-caption-cluster paired-bootstrap intervals after averaging seed deltas. Directional lower bounds above zero for both wire zeroing and same-modality wire derangement would show a positive task contribution from the sample-specific signal to these fixed deployments. An embedding change establishes numerical dependence. A task-specific effect at fixed weights is distinct from an architectural advantage against independently retrained controls.
