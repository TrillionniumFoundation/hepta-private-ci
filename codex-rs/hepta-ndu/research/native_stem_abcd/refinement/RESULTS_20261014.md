## Fixed object and controlled allocation changes

R10 keeps the same continuous population, known affine plant, observed Markov preference transition, nonlinear shared forward Stem, quartic terminal loss and entropic recursive cost. Widths 64 and 256 correspond to 498 and 1,842 trainable parameters. The physical state, three-step horizon and task are not scaled.

The 96-case factorial crosses two widths, three seeds, four solvers, two training schedules and two monitoring allocations. Within every width/seed, all sixteen conditions start from byte-identical parameters.

- `fixed_gl5`: optimize the 625-node GL5 objective for the complete 12-second fitting budget.
- `stage_gl3_gl5`: optimize the 81-node GL3 objective for the first four seconds, then restart the same solver class from the latest accepted tensor on GL5 for the remaining fitting budget.
- `frequent_lazy`: record the exact short-circuit CC5/CC9 gate during fitting, but never stop or select a checkpoint.
- `terminal_only`: spend no fitting budget on intermediate qualification.

Every candidate is frozen after the fitting budget and then receives the same full terminal gate outside that budget:

```text
max(J_CC5, J_CC9) <= 0.04850
and abs(J_CC9 - J_CC5) <= 1e-6
```

Terminal qualification cannot change the frozen tensor or resume fitting. Consequently this round compares final endpoint quality, not first-observed time-to-quality.

## Primary result: staged integration improves all large-width dense costs, but the old gate still passes none

Primary was fixed at width 256 with terminal-only qualification: staged GL3→GL5 versus fixed GL5, separately for all methods and seeds. The staged schedule lowers CC17 cost in **all 12/12 pairs**, while both conditions remain **0/12 qualified**.

| Method | Fixed GL5 mean CC17 | Staged mean CC17 | Mean staged-minus-fixed | Paired improvements | Final qualifications |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| adam | 0.049031480054 | 0.0486359884556 | -0.000395491598414 | 3/3 | 0/3 vs 0/3 |
| lbfgs | 0.048545564535 | 0.0484309340342 | -0.00011463050084 | 3/3 | 0/3 vs 0/3 |
| adjoint_ncg | 0.0485018036878 | 0.0484357199075 | -6.6083780346e-05 | 3/3 | 0/3 vs 0/3 |
| ad_ncg | 0.0484968222024 | 0.0484225762576 | -7.42459448092e-05 | 3/3 | 0/3 vs 0/3 |

The largest gains are visible in the matrix-free and L-BFGS candidates, whose staged means move close to or below the cost threshold. The old CC5/CC9 discrepancy condition nevertheless rejects every large-width candidate. Thus R10 alleviates optimization error without satisfying the inherited numerical-qualification rule.

This is not a contradiction: the staged objective ends on GL5, whereas qualification still requires the fixed CC5 rule to agree with CC9. Better optimization of a more accurate finite objective does not force a particular coarse rule to become accurate.

One contrary result is retained: staged Adam is not uniformly safe. A large-width frequent-monitor seed degrades materially, even though the staged mean under terminal-only monitoring improves. Low-accuracy early optimization is therefore not an unconditional continuation method.

## Secondary result: terminal-only allocation saves fitting budget, with solver-dependent endpoint effects

At width 256 under fixed GL5, terminal-only removes all intermediate qualification work while keeping the 12-second fitting budget unchanged. No condition qualifies under either monitor allocation. Endpoint effects are modest and method-dependent:

Freeing monitor time usually permits more optimizer work, but does not guarantee a better endpoint. Explicit-adjoint trust-ncg produces identical frozen tensors in several fixed-rule pairs because the saved time is insufficient to complete another accepted update. This prevents interpreting “zero monitor time” as an automatic model-quality gain.

## Interpretation and delivery boundary

R10 shows that staged integration can improve every paired large-width dense endpoint, and that removing intermediate qualification can return compute to optimization. It does **not** turn the large-width group into a qualified model under the old gate. The remaining failure is now more specifically attributed: optimization is materially improved, while the fixed CC5/CC9 agreement rule remains the binding criterion for many low-cost candidates.

This does not establish universal NDU superiority, a global optimum, unknown-world learning or an LLM/world-model result.
