## One actual continuous target, not train/test population substitution

Keep R5's 78-parameter nonlinear feedback Stem, known affine plant, quartic
terminal task errors and entropic recursive cost (eta=.4, gamma=.93, horizon 3).
Fix the initial law to independent uniforms h in [-.25,.25]^2 and x in [-.8,.8]^2,
with two equiprobable starting regimes. Conditional branches are exactly summed.
All methods target this SAME continuous law. There is no pretrained encoder,
unknown-world fit, independently learned preference law or general FBSDE theorem.

CC3 and CC5 tensor Clenshaw-Curtis rules use 81 and 625 initial points with
positive integration weights. They approximate the same continuous integral;
knowing the law is not the same as performing exact integration. The old
16-corner rule is explicitly a biased coverage control. Initial tensors,
quadrature states and weights, parameter family, scalar objective and nominal
8-second CPU cap match within each B/C pair. Weighted root mass is w_i/2,
propagated with recursive-risk tilted reach. Replacing it by uniform sample
counts is tested and rejected. Explicit gradients and Hessians agree with
ordinary AD and finite differences.

Primary: nonlinear CC5 adjoint/trust-exact C versus Adam B, no ridge, all three
seeds. Ordinary-AD trust-exact, L-BFGS and full-gradient roots are controls.
The separately fixed ridge .5*.001*mean(theta^2) changes the training objective;
its results are not pooled with the primary. Small matrix/scalar and from-scratch
Transformer controls have different capacities and priors.

## Coverage helps; denser integration is not free

Moving C from the corner surrogate to CC3 reduces same-parent cost from .119603
to .048459. In CC3, C beats Adam for two seeds and loses slightly for the third;
it is not an all-seed superiority result. Under the fixed CPU cap, moving to
CC5 increases C's cost to .049873; Adam also worsens slightly from CC3 to CC5.
The added quadrature work is real. A denser rule can reduce integration bias
while leaving fewer numerical optimization steps; density alone is not success.

The simple ridge does not repair the primary result: CC5 unregularized evaluation
costs are .048689778920 for ridge-Adam and .049890294351 for ridge-C, both slightly
worse than their respective no-ridge averages. This one fixed coefficient does
not rule out other regularizers, and its coordinate dependence is explicit.

## Integration diagnostics, not fabricated exactness

For every frozen candidate, evaluate CC3/5/9/17, independent GL9, and four
scrambled Sobol replicates of 512 points. CC17 has 83,521 initial states;
CC9/GL9 each have 6,561. All points still enumerate both regimes and all branches.
The four Sobol sets use the same parent law and fixed seeds, not four new tasks.
The evaluator never feeds results back to fitting.

For the SIX primary B/C models, the largest |J_CC17-J_CC9| is 8.71e-10 and
largest |J_CC17-J_GL9| is 1.06e-13. Their QMC mean offsets from CC17 are small
relative to the .000927--.001407 paired differences. The ranking is supported by
several numerical rules, not just an internal training residual.

This accuracy does NOT extend uniformly to all saved models: the corner-C
seed23 has |J_CC17-J_CC9|=.00137423 and |J_CC17-J_GL9|=.00114175. Its sharp
policy is harder to integrate. These discrepancies are retained, not hidden
by reporting only primary models. Refinement agreement is not a certified
bound on actual integral error, and four-scramble SE is not a guaranteed CI.

A conditional error budget clarifies the target: if uniformly over a fixed
parameter set |J_Q-J_mu|<=epsilon_Q and a solver attains J_Q(theta)<=inf J_Q+delta,
then J_mu(theta)-inf J_mu<=2 epsilon_Q+delta. This follows by adding/subtracting
J_Q at theta and at an approximate population minimizer. Neither this uniform
quadrature bound nor a global delta certificate was established for these
nonconvex neural fits; observed quadrature differences cannot replace them.
