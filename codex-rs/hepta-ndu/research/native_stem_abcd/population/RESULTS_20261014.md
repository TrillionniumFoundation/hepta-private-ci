## This is now a forward representation learning problem

Let s=(h,x), h in R^3, with an observed two-regime preference state p. The
learned action u=K_(k,p)s changes h_(k+1) through s_next=F s+G u+n_p epsilon.
There are 108 trainable state-transition/control weights over three layers and
two regimes. They are shared across every task, input and branch, not tied
across layers. Five fixed readouts of h_T are compared to R_m T x with a fixed
nondiagonal, nonscalar target T. No pretrained embedding or target-optimal gains
are provided to the fitted arms. All parameter groups receive actual gradients.

The objective is a known, preference-modulated CONDITIONAL QUADRATIC recursive
cost with gamma=.93 and known finite Markov/noise law. This is a restricted NDU
special case, not R3's nonlinear entropic generator, learned endogenous
preference dynamics, a continuous-time stochastic maximum-principle theorem,
or natural-language training. The 64 input/state sign combinations, two initial
regimes and all four transitions at each step are the complete finite training
population (128 initial cases, 8192 leaves). New continuous-valued initial states
are outside that finite training law. The underlying linear target/dynamics
remain supplied by the same known generator.

For a fixed candidate K, backward P describes its actual continuation cost,
and forward conditional second moments M describe its actual state occupancy.
Writing A=F+GK, Pbar=E[P_next|p], the full population derivative is

    E_(k,p)=R_p K_(k,p)+gamma G' Pbar_(k,p) A_(k,p),
    dJ/dK_(k,p)=2 gamma^k E_(k,p) M_(k,p).

C solves the coupled reduced equations E=0 over the shared K. This is not
regression against an already-solved value formula. M-positive-definiteness is
needed for the converse equivalence to zero population gradient and was checked
at the solution (minimum conditional eigenvalue about .001918). Backward values
are eliminated/recomputed at each candidate, rather than optimized freely per
sample. The method exploits the exact quadratic moment closure; it is not a
solver for general neural dynamics. A full-gradient-root control retains the M
factors and tests whether the gains come from conditioning rather than a new
objective. B/C share the same class, initial bytes, full objective and information.

D independently computes Riccati coefficients. An independently assembled
63-variable adapted-action stochastic-tree quadratic program checks its cost
and initial action for both starting regimes. Positive action Hessians and
completion of squares verify the global optimum in this finite LQ problem.
Ordinary FP64 checks are not interval arithmetic certificates. No fitted arm
calls D or the independent action optimizer during training.

Primary metric is new-initial-state population cost minus the independent
optimal cost. The optimum there is 0.20111738336688798 (rounding may affect the
last digit). Negative gaps around 1e-16 are floating-point cancellation, NOT
beating the optimum. All three seeds are retained; no budget or winner was
chosen from held-out performance.

At the registered eight-second cap, matrix Adam AND C are at the same optimal
objective within numerical precision. Therefore the primary is a TIE, not a
claimed C generalization victory. At two seconds Adam's mean new-state gap is
.00100309629 while all C fits are already at the optimum. C's eight-second-cap
fits terminate in .722405--.727297 CPU seconds. Its gain coefficients agree with
D to at most 2.2204460493e-16. This establishes parameter recovery for an actual
forward Stem in this special case and a bounded efficiency observation.

Full-gradient-root also reaches the optimum, in .939586--1.043026 CPU seconds.
Thus the advantage is NOT unique to an NDU-branded objective: the explicit
adjoint equations provide a useful conditioning/structure reduction of the same
population optimization. D itself computes coefficients in roughly .000295 CPU
seconds, excluding its separately paid evaluation. No neural method beats that
analytic reference on its own solvable task.

Adam/L-BFGS are the preregistered fixed-budget implementations, without a
convergence early-stop. Their eight-second totals are not measurements of the
FIRST time they reached a target quality. No sleep, fake work, or budget-padding
operation was inserted, but no exact 11x time-to-solution speedup is claimed.
The Transformer is a real from-scratch attention network with 683 parameters,
not capacity matched to the 108-parameter matrix family. One small architecture,
one learning rate, and eight CPU seconds do not establish a general Transformer
limitation. Matrix algorithms can exploit exact moment closure; attention uses
full enumeration of the SAME finite law, at higher computation cost.

## Scalar shortcuts are excluded in a defined class

Even allowing one scalar per layer AND regime (six scalars total), u=alpha*(x-h)
cannot attain the unrestricted optimum. The performance-difference identity
lower-bounds total regret by its first-step regret. Minimizing that first-step
quadratic over alpha separately for each regime gives a training-population
objective-gap lower bound 0.004206282058058591. The bound applies to ALL policies
in that declared scalar class, not just a particular failed fit; it does not
exclude every imaginable one-parameter parametrization. Its formula is analytic
and its reported value numerically evaluated, not outward-rounded certified.
A 36-parameter diagonal-control class is also retained and performs materially
worse than full matrix controls in this run. Off-diagonal representation mixing
is genuinely used; no scalar entropy-slope calibration solves this task.

## A zero gradient can be a saddle; solver status is not a certificate

Post-seal Hessian inspection was not used for training or model selection.
The fitted scalar gradient-root has max population gradient
1.66533453694e-16 but minimum Hessian eigenvalue -0.431700486988: a saddle, not
an optimum. Its new-state gap .550174 is worse than scalar Adam's .483951.
The diagonal root reports numerical optimizer termination but leaves max TRUE
population gradient .00175568549 and minimum Hessian eigenvalue -.0414097418.
It minimized the root residual without solving the original stationary system.
Its failure remains in the scientific readout despite successful code execution.

By contrast, the three full-matrix gradient roots have positive Hessian minimum
about .0037567563 and are independently verified against D. Neither successful
least-squares termination nor a small equation/gradient residual should alone
be used to promote an unrestricted future NDU model. Root residual, second-order
conditions and independent objective/verification bounds have separate roles.

## Transfer scope and an important retained failure

New states, 3x state magnitudes, correlated h/x and 3x additive noise preserve the
original optimal matrix gains in this quadratic setting; near-zero gaps there
are expected from the correctly recovered feedback law, not proof of arbitrary
out-of-distribution language generalization. The seven new readouts are new
linear combinations of the same latent target, not unrelated semantic tasks.
Raw task MSE is reported alongside resource-sensitive cost, and need not be
minimized by the cost-optimal policy. There is no learned probability forecast,
so calibration is explicitly not applicable rather than assigned a fake score.

Frozen C gains do NOT stay optimal under arbitrary changed problems: mean gap
.0131466391 when F changes, .00209397015 when preference transitions change,
and .3216743551 for horizon four under the declared modulo-three layer reuse.
D is recomputed for changed problems and is an adaptive reference, not a frozen
learned competitor. Extending the horizon is not covered by the original
three-step solution guarantee. All of these negative results are preserved.

## Next scientific question

The next extension should replace this exactly closed quadratic profile with
nonquadratic recursive costs or nonlinear shared forward dynamics, retain a
strong complete-gradient root baseline, and explicitly verify local-versus-global
solutions. The present result supports direct recovery of forward weights in a
checkable multivariate special case; it does NOT show inevitable generalization
superiority over sufficiently optimized conventional training. That next nonlinear
extension has not been executed in this report.
