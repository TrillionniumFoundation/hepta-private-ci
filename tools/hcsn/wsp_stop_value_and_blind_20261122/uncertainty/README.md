# Checkpoint editing under fee and transition uncertainty

These audits use the original five renewal-policy checkpoints. All prices, transition probabilities and terminal utilities in the math experiments belong to the archived finite task environment. The research does not promote a market policy.

* `ambiguity/study.py` compares one task-conditioned three-bias edit, the same scalar optimized with Adam, transferred edits, and native policy operators under a common fee multiplier. Two endpoint differences suffice to evaluate uniform improvement of a fixed policy. The source network is reloaded after writing each selected model.
* `ambiguity/qualify.py` evaluates the same edit family and separate native policy solvers under rectangular uncertainty. Every action can independently tilt its ordered branch probabilities, and every branch can independently vary its fee. The verifier supplies an attaining adversary and checks an independently solved bound at every state. An incumbent-relative criterion admits sparse direct edits.
* `transfer/root_only.py` tests selection from the initial representation only. Six representations/controls and a fixed five-action displacement grid use 160 fits and 80 validation worlds. The selected recipes and prospective test predictions are sealed before values on 80 new schedules are computed.
* The associated records contain method commitments and input/output digests. Full schedules, rows, candidate artifacts, original source data and Linux worker binaries are in the accompanying access-controlled research evidence. Exact replay requires the original sealed binaries or a byte-matching build, since their digests enter the schedule-generation seals.

On 80 new tasks under the broader uncertainty bounds, 42 sparse edits strictly improved the incumbent, and 38 retained it. A pointwise-dominating action policy improved 57. The absolute maximin policy reduced value in at least one replay law on 19 tasks. On a separate fresh set, the representation-only method selected on validation improved 15, regressed on 15 and tied on 50; a zero-validation-loss selector kept the base model. Bounds require the stated state graph, admissible actions, rewards and uncertainty set. The runtime implementation and public CLI fixtures are reviewed in TrillionniumFoundation/hepta-runtime#153.
